About Liliw

Liliw, officially the Municipality of Liliw, is a charming 4th class municipality in the province of Laguna, Philippines. Known as the "Tsinelas Capital of the Philippines," Liliw is famous for its handcrafted footwear industry, particularly its colorful and intricately designed slippers and sandals.

Beyond its thriving footwear industry, Liliw is blessed with natural wonders and rich cultural heritage. The municipality is home to stunning waterfalls, historic churches, ancestral houses, and vibrant festivals that showcase the warmth and creativity of its people. The town's Spanish colonial architecture, particularly the Liliw Church (San Juan Bautista Parish Church) built in 1646, stands as a testament to its historical significance.

Visitors to Liliw can enjoy a perfect blend of adventure, culture, and relaxation. From trekking to the beautiful waterfalls in the uplands to exploring the heritage houses in the town proper, and shopping for unique footwear products at affordable prices, Liliw offers an authentic Filipino experience that captures the heart of Laguna's rural charm.

Helpful Information

Getting There

From Manila:

  • Take a bus to Santa Cruz, Laguna (via SLEX)
  • From Santa Cruz, take a jeepney or van to Liliw
  • Travel time: 2.5 - 3 hours

By Private Vehicle:

  • Via SLEX, exit at Calamba
  • Follow signs to Liliw via National Road
  • Approximately 100 km from Manila

Best Time to Visit

Peak Season:

  • January (Liliw Festival)
  • Holy Week (March/April)
  • Summer months (April-May)

Weather:

  • Dry season: November to April
  • Wet season: May to October
  • Cool months: December to February

What to Bring

Essentials:

  • Comfortable walking shoes
  • Light, breathable clothing
  • Sunscreen and hat
  • Insect repellent
  • Reusable water bottle

For Waterfall Tours:

  • Swimwear and towel
  • Waterproof bag
  • Change of clothes
  • Trekking shoes

Local Tips

  • Bring cash - limited ATMs available
  • Respect local customs and traditions
  • Try local delicacies like uraro and pilipit
  • Shop for footwear at the public market
  • Hire local guides for waterfall tours
  • Practice responsible tourism
  • Learn basic Filipino phrases
